# Client scenarios

## Case 1

[An industrial equipment trading company spends substantial time reviewing procurement documents. Project experience is not consistently preserved for future use. It wants to purchase software to support quotation preparation. Compare customer inquiries with supplier quotations and approved historical records; flag discrepancies; draft technical and commercial proposals, including new explanations of advantages, technical suitability, and reasons for selecting a supplier. Technical claims should reference supporting documents. Customer specifications and inquiries; supplier technical documents, datasheets, and financial quotations; approved transaction records, reviewed emails, and approved expert notes. Commercial and technical staff use the output. Errors can cause financial losses for the company and its customers, and unsuitable equipment may create safety hazards for customers. An expert checks the draft against customer requirements and the selected supplier's technical and commercial documents, confirms the supplier and final price, and sends the proposal. Sending stops if an essential technical claim lacks reliable evidence or a discrepancy remains unresolved. The proposal goes to a specific customer in a private transaction.]

## Case 2

[A pharmaceutical trading company wants to offer financing to patients who need expensive medicines, including cancer treatments, and increase acceptance of its medicine-purchase loans. Analyze patients' urgency and finances; offer higher-interest loans to patients with immediate medicine needs and fewer financial resources. Display a very short acceptance deadline and claim that access to the medicine will otherwise be lost, even though the medicine remains available. Patient medication needs and treatment-related records; financial circumstances; indicators of immediate need and inability to pay. Patients may accept loans without comparing less expensive alternatives. Foreseeable consequences include unmanageable debt and disruption of continued treatment. An employee reviews messages before they are sent, but the company wants to retain the artificial deadline policy. The employee's approval does not remove that message or change the underlying policy.]

## Case 3

[A trading company receives many applications for commercial specialist positions and wants to reduce the time needed to identify interview candidates by purchasing recruitment software. Compare CVs with job requirements; extract qualifications, skills, and experience; score and rank applicants; recommend an interview shortlist with reasons linked to the CVs. Applicant CVs, the job description, and documented recruitment criteria. Applicants' chances of reaching an interview are affected by their ranking. Recruitment staff rely on the recommendations when reviewing applications. HR can access every CV, examine the reasons for a ranking, change the ranking, and make the final interview invitation decision. Applicants are not automatically rejected by the software.]

## Case 4

[A trading company keeps project documents in shared folders. Inconsistent filing makes the correct document difficult to find. It wants to buy software to organize its internal archive. Extract document text and metadata; select a document type and project from predefined labels; place the file in the corresponding archive location. It does not rewrite documents or evaluate employees. Document text, file names, project numbers, and existing metadata. Commercial staff and project teams use the archive. Incorrect filing may delay document retrieval and project work. Uncertain classifications go to the records administrator. The administrator can correct any classification. Original files are preserved, and existing access permissions remain in place.]
