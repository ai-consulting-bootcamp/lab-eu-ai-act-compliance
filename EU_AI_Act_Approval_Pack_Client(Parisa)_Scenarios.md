 `Case 1 - Prohibited`
 AI-Powered Shoplifting Risk Detection
 A large supermarket chain wants to reduce shoplifting in its stores. It proposes an AI system that analyzes CCTV footage, facial expressions, body language, and customer movements to identify shoppers who appear likely to steal. Customers receiving a high-risk score would be automatically flagged to security staff for closer monitoring. The system would also build profiles from previous visits. Security staff can view the alerts, but the AI determines who should be considered suspicious.

 `Case 2 - High-risk`
 AI Recruitment Candidate Screening
 A recruitment agency wants to speed up screening for graduate jobs. Candidates upload their CVs, qualifications, work history, and answers to application questions. An AI system scores each applicant and recommends which candidates should proceed to an interview. Recruiters review the shortlist but normally follow the AI ranking unless they notice an obvious error. Job applicants are directly affected because low-ranked candidates may not progress.


 `Case 3 - Limited risk / transparency`
 AI Customer Support Chatbot
 An online travel company wants to provide 24/7 customer support using a generative AI chatbot. The chatbot answers questions about bookings, baggage rules, cancellations, and destinations using the customer's booking information and the company's support documentation. Customers interact directly with the chatbot before being transferred to a human agent for complex problems. The company wants the bot to communicate naturally so that conversations feel similar to speaking with a support employee.


 `Case 4 - Minimal risk`

 AI Product Description Generator
 A small furniture retailer wants AI to help its marketing team write product descriptions for its online store. Employees enter basic information such as dimensions, materials, colour, and product features. The AI generates a suggested description, which a marketing employee reviews and edits before publishing. The AI does not rank customers, make eligibility decisions, or determine prices. Only the retailer's internal marketing team uses the system.
