Case 1 - Prohibited
A retail bank wants an AI tool to score loan applicants using social media activity and browsing history, not just financial data. The tool would flag "high risk" applicants and auto-reject them without human review. The bank wants faster approvals and fewer bad loans.

Why prohibited: this is social scoring style profiling that leads to detrimental treatment, based on data unrelated to the context, with no human review. This crosses into Article 5 territory.

Case 2 - High-risk
A hospital network wants an AI tool to help triage patients in the ER. The tool reads vitals and symptoms and suggests a priority level. A nurse can override the suggestion before it's acted on.

Why high-risk: this affects health and safety decisions. It falls under Annex III high-risk medical/health use. Human oversight exists, but the system still needs conformity checks, logging, and documentation.

Case 3 - Limited risk / transparency
An e-commerce company wants a chatbot on their site to answer order and return questions. The bot sounds natural and some customers think they're talking to a real person.

Why transparency: the AI interacts with humans directly. Article 50 requires disclosure that it's an AI system. No high-risk decision is made, so this stays limited risk with a disclosure obligation.

Case 4 - Minimal risk
A small design agency wants an AI tool that suggests color palettes and font pairings for client mockups. Designers review and approve every suggestion before use.

Why minimal: no safety, rights, or high-stakes decision involved. No specific AI Act obligation applies, though normal IP and client contract concerns still apply.
