## Client reaction

**Case 1:** Accepted. Partner confirmed the trust score was designed to fail Article 5(1)(c), including the detail that it applies to private companies, not just government use. Redesign to a transparent, reward-only loyalty tier stands.

**Case 2:** Accepted. Partner flagged the same gap I did, that "a human sees the rest" doesn't cover the auto-rejected 70%, so Article 14 oversight is missing for that group. Controls stand: recruiter spot-checks rejections, logging, bias audit before launch.

**Case 3:** Accepted. Partner's brief centered on Article 50 exactly as I called it. Disclosure fix stands, no further changes needed.

**Case 4:** Accepted. Partner confirmed no dynamic pricing and no legal/significant profiling effect, which keeps it minimal risk. GDPR/ePrivacy flag for the privacy notice stands.

## Where the hidden scenario worked well

All four cases were realistic enough to infer the category from business context alone, no case needed a guess. Case 1 worked especially well: the detail about sharing the score with a logistics partner made the "unrelated context" element of Article 5(1)(c) clear without stating it outright.

## Where it was too vague

None of the four required a follow-up question to classify. If anything, Case 2 could have hidden the tell a bit more, "a human always makes the final hiring decision" was close to a direct hint toward the Article 14 gap.

## Closing note: what changed after the client discussion

Nothing changed in the classification or the decisions. All four calls matched the intended answer key exactly. The discussion confirmed the reasoning behind each call rather than correcting it, in particular the Article 5(1)(c) private-company scope on Case 1 and the Article 14 oversight gap on Case 2. The approval pack stands as written.
