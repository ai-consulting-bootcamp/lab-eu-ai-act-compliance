## Workflow
1. The partner wrote four hidden client briefs for a logistics company.
2. I classified each case without her answer key.
3. I proposed an architecture, roles and controls for each case.
4. I issued a decision for each case.
5. She revealed her key, and we compared her intended categories with mine.

## One run: input, evidence, output
- **Input:** the partner's four briefs: ranking job applications; detecting frustration in call-centre staff; a parcel-tracking chatbot; labelling empty boxes.
- **Evidence:** the legal basis for each call: Annex III point 4(a); Art. 5(1)(f); Art. 50(1) and (5); and no Art. 5 practice or Annex III area for case 4.
- **Output:**
  - Case 1: high-risk, approve with controls.
  - Case 2: prohibited, deny and redesign.
  - Case 3: transparency, approve with controls.
  - Case 4: minimal risk, approve.
- **Verification:** the partner confirmed on 17 Nov 2026 that all four inferred categories matched her answer key.

## Failure / limitation
- **The client discussion was short and written.** On Slack (17 Nov 2026, 17:10), Sandra accepted my four recommendations and challenged none. In the other direction, she proposed replacing my Case 2 with an Annex III example, and I accepted (16:56).
- **The briefs left implementation details open.** Ranking weights, chatbot authentication and error rates for the vision model are not described. That is why two cases are "approve with controls" rather than "approve".
