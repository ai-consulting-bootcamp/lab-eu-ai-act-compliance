# Phase 1 — My four hidden client cases and private answer key

**Author:** Jaime Garate Marquerie · **Shared with:** Sandra Obregón (the briefs) · **Source:** `Classify_Your_Product_4_Casos_para_Companera.docx`, translated from Spanish. Content unchanged.

## The four client briefs (as given to my partner)

### Case 1 — An independent bookshop
An independent bookshop wants to spend less time researching and selecting books. It proposes an AI agent that researches authors, publishers and new releases, using public catalogues, publishers' pages and the bookseller's own searches. From that information it produces dossiers with synopses, edition data and buying recommendations. The results will influence which titles customers find, especially in fiction and essays. The bookseller reviews the sources and personally decides which books to stock and recommend.

### Case 2 — A smart-toy factory: worker appraisal and promotion (final version)
A company that manufactures smart toys wants to speed up the quarterly appraisals of its assembly staff. It proposes using AI to analyse each worker's production and quality-control records, such as units assembled and parts that reached inspection with defects. The system produces an individual score and recommends whom to consider for promotion to line supervisor. The supervisor reviews the recommendation, can consult the records and decides which candidate to propose to HR.

*Revision history:* my first Case 2 was a motorised ride-on toy for 18-month-olds with AI braking (Art. 6(1) + Annex I). On 17 Nov 2026 (Slack, 16:51) Sandra suggested replacing it with the case above so that the high-risk example follows the Annex III pattern of the lab, and I accepted (16:56). The text above is her proposed wording, translated.

### Case 3 — A music production company
A production company organises concerts and must decide which musicians to hire for its next seasons. It proposes an AI that analyses video and audio recordings of rehearsals to assess performers. The system examines facial expressions and voice features to estimate nervousness, frustration, enthusiasm and motivation. The results are combined into an individual score that helps decide contract renewals and new hires. The musicians will know the system exists but cannot change the scores it generates. The musical director reviews the scores before deciding who continues with the company.

### Case 4 — A specialist tea shop
A tea shop wants to answer customer questions outside opening hours. It proposes adding a virtual assistant to its website that chats with visitors and recommends teas. The system uses the catalogue, supplier information and customers' written questions to generate personalised answers about origins, flavours, ingredients and preparation. The assistant appears under the brand name "Tea Guide", without initially saying whether answers come from a person or an automated system. It makes no medical diagnoses and does not complete purchases by itself; an employee handles queries that need review.

## Private answer key

| Case | Intended category | Legal basis | What the case tests |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 · Bookshop | **Minimal risk** | No Art. 5 practice; not an Annex III area; not an Art. 6(1) safety component | Based on my own Project 3: shows that "minimal" still needs justification, plus GDPR and copyright as parallel issues |
| 2 · Smart-toy factory, promotion | **High-risk** | **Art. 6(2) + Annex III 4(b)**: AI used to evaluate work performance and support promotion decisions; the score influences who is proposed, even though the supervisor decides | Tests that a human final decision does not remove the Annex III classification |
| 3 · Music producer | **Prohibited** | **Art. 5(1)(f)**: emotion inference in the workplace; informing workers and human review do not lift the ban | Tests that "a human reviews it" does not save a prohibited practice |
| 4 · Tea shop | **Limited risk / transparency** | **Art. 50(1) and (5)**: direct interaction; the name alone does not make it obvious that it is AI | Tests the AI-notice duty at the first interaction |

## Partner's result (debrief)

- Sandra's categories matched my intended categories for all four cases.
- Case 2 was revised during the exchange: Sandra proposed the factory version, classified it High-risk under Art. 6(2) and Annex III 4(b), and I accepted it (17 Nov 2026).
- Her comments on the other three cases confirmed Minimal risk (bookshop), Prohibited under Art. 5(1)(f) (music producer) and Transparency under Art. 50(1) and (5) (tea shop).
- The same revised Case 2 is the case carried forward to the GDPR lab *Same product, privacy lens*.
