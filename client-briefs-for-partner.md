# EU AI Act Approval Pack - Ben Mayer

## Consulting response

Client briefs for the blind review. All companies are fictional. This is the version the partner sees, with no labels and cases in random order. Read each one as a normal client request and flag any assumptions you'd confirm on a follow-up call.

### Case W

- A Danish publishing house owns roughly 400,000 digitised photos from 1950 to today, but most have no useful keywords, so editors cannot find images for new books.
- It wants to send the images in batches to a vendor's image-recognition API that suggests keywords (landscape, building type, decade, objects, colours) and a one-line caption draft.
- Suggested keywords and captions land in the internal archive search as "pending"; two archivists accept or reject them in bulk and can edit any caption.
- Editors use the search to pick images for book layouts.
- Many of the historical photos show people at work, at events and on the street.
- The publisher wants a contract that stops the vendor from keeping or training on the images.

### Case X

- A Spanish fashion chain with 14 city-centre stores says its new entrance screens "show the same campaign to everyone" and convert poorly.
- It wants to license a retail-analytics camera package that looks at each shopper's face as they walk in and picks which campaign the screen plays next.
- The vendor's "cultural profile" feature segments shoppers from their facial features and clothing (such as head coverings) alongside age band and gender, so the modest-wear and festive collections reach "the right audience".
- Faces are not stored or matched to identities; only the estimated segments are logged per hour for the marketing team's weekly report.
- Shoppers, including tourists and children with their parents, are the people analysed. No one in the store reviews the screen choice in real time.
- The marketing director wants a pilot in three stores before the autumn season.

### Case Y

- A regional tourism board in the Algarve wants a summer social-media campaign that feels personal, but its budget does not stretch to a film crew for 30 short videos.
- Its creative agency proposes "Marta", a friendly local guide who presents hidden beaches, markets and family restaurants in each clip.
- Marta's face, gestures and voice would be generated with a third-party video-generation platform; the voice is based on a recording of a voice actor who signed a licence for this campaign.
- Some clips would show real beaches and towns, partly re-rendered by the same platform with sunnier weather and fewer people.
- Viewers are potential tourists across Europe scrolling Instagram and TikTok; the board's marketing team approves every clip before it is posted.
- The board would publish under its own account; the agency operates the platform and delivers finished files.

### Case Z

- A private business school in Vienna receives about 3,000 applications for 400 places on its English-taught bachelor's programme, and its four admissions officers cannot read everything before the offer deadline.
- It wants to license an EdTech vendor's tool that reads each motivation essay and the transcript of a recorded video interview, scores them against the school's rubric, and combines that with school-leaving grades into one admission score.
- Applicants above a top threshold get a conditional offer email automatically; applicants below a lower threshold are moved to the waiting list automatically; the middle band is read by an officer.
- Most applicants are 17 to 19 and many are non-native English speakers applying from outside Austria.
- Officers can change any score, but the school expects them to only open the middle band during peak weeks.
- The vendor hosts the tool and trains its scoring model on past admission decisions from several partner schools.
