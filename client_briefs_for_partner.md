# EU AI Act Approval Pack – Client Briefs (Nicolò → Friedrich)

Four client requests from discovery calls. Each company asked: **"Can we launch this?"**
Please review them as the consultant: classify each case, propose the architecture and role map, and give your decision.

## Case 1 – Callwerk Services GmbH

We're a customer-service outsourcing company with about 400 agents in Berlin and Lisbon. We handle inbound calls for telecom and insurance clients. Staff turnover is 45% a year, and our clients complain that call quality is inconsistent. We want an AI tool that listens to live calls and uses the agent's webcam to read tone of voice and facial expressions, flagging when someone is frustrated, stressed or disengaged. Each agent would get a daily "mood and engagement" score on a team-lead dashboard. Team leads would use it in weekly coaching and when deciding who gets the premium client accounts and the preferred shifts. We plan to present it to agents as a wellbeing tool. Team leads can overrule the score, but realistically most won't have time.

## Case 2 – Paylo GmbH

We're a Berlin fintech offering "pay later" at checkout for around 300 online fashion and electronics shops. Defaults went from 2% to 5% last year, mostly among first-time customers. We want a model that decides in under two seconds whether a shopper can pay later, and what their spending limit is. It would use checkout data (basket, merchant, time of day), device and location signals, the customer's repayment history with us and, if the customer connects their bank account, 90 days of transactions. Declines would be fully automatic. Our operations team only looks at a case if the customer complains. We'd license the base model from a specialist vendor and retrain it on our own data.

## Case 3 – Radhaus Brandt

We're a family-run e-bike retailer with four stores around Brandenburg and a growing online shop. In spring our phone line is overwhelmed, and we lose bookings because nobody picks up. We want an assistant that answers calls and WhatsApp messages 24/7. It would book service appointments, check repair status and answer warranty questions using our manuals and price lists. Our founder Jonas recorded an hour of audio so the assistant can speak in his voice. We'd also use it to make short Instagram videos where "Jonas" presents new models, so he doesn't have to film them. Refunds and complaints get passed to a staff member the next morning. Ideally customers just feel they're talking to us, as they always have.

## Case 4 – Nordholz Baustoffe GmbH

We're a building-materials wholesaler with 12 depots in northern Germany, selling mainly to contractors and tradespeople. We regularly run out of key items like insulation and drywall, while slow-moving stock ties up cash. We want a forecasting tool that predicts demand per product and per depot for the next six weeks and suggests reorder quantities. It would use five years of sales history, weather forecasts, regional building-permit statistics and supplier lead times. Buyers at each depot would see the suggestions and approve or edit every purchase order. Depot managers would also like to use the weekly volume forecast to decide how many temporary warehouse workers to book through our staffing agency.
