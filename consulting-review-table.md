# Consulting review: partner cases A–D (blind first pass)

Reviewer: Ben Mayer. Source: partner briefs at [`s-rostovskyi/lab-eu-ai-act-compliance/client-briefs.md`](https://github.com/s-rostovskyi/lab-eu-ai-act-compliance/blob/main/client-briefs.md).
I wrote this table **before** opening the partner's answer key. The comparison is in [`debrief-and-client-discussion.md`](./debrief-and-client-discussion.md).

| Case | Likely category | Why this is my first-pass call | Proposed AI architecture | Provider / deployer / vendor | Required obligations or controls | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A** - Belgian rail operator, timetable/accessibility chat widget | **Limited risk (transparency)** | A system that talks directly to members of the public falls under Art 50(1). It is a passenger-information tool, not a safety component in managing rail traffic, so Annex III §2 (critical infrastructure) doesn't apply. It makes no decisions about people (tickets and compensation stay with staff). | **Trigger:** passenger types a question. **Behaviour:** retrieval over approved timetable and accessibility pages, then an LLM answers only from what it retrieved and cites the source link. **Human point:** staff own the source pages; a "can't confirm, talk to staff" handover fires when sources are missing or conflict; staff review a sample of logs each week. **Output:** short answer + link. **Disclosure/logging:** "You're chatting with an AI assistant" banner; conversation logs with retention limit. | **Provider:** the rail operator, because it builds the widget and puts it into service under its own brand. **Deployer:** also the rail operator. **Vendor:** the LLM API company is a general-purpose AI (GPAI) model provider (Art 53 documentation to downstream providers). | Art 50(1) AI disclosure, built in by the operator as provider. Art 50(2) machine-readable marking of generated text (verify with legal whether the grounded-answer setup counts as "assistive"). Art 4 AI literacy for the staff who maintain it. Parallel: GDPR for chat logs (passengers may type health/disability info), so minimise and limit retention; accuracy risk for step-free routes, so show freshness dates and send all access questions to a source link. | **Approve with controls** |
| **B** - German staffing agency, vendor tool that ranks applicants | **High-risk** | Annex III §4(a) covers AI used to analyse and filter applications and evaluate candidates. Ranking people is profiling, so the Art 6(3) "no significant risk" exemption can't apply. Recruiters starting at the top 20 means the rank effectively decides who gets an interview. | **Trigger:** new vacancy + applications. **Behaviour:** vendor model scores CV, availability and certificate against job requirements and shows job-related reasons per candidate. **Human point:** recruiter reviews and invites; must log a reason when overriding; minimum-qualified candidates are shown in **bands** instead of a strict 1..n order to cut anchoring on the top 20. **Output:** shortlist + reasons; never auto-rejects. **Logging:** automatic logs of ranks, overrides and invitations. | **Provider:** the vendor (places a branded high-risk system on the market). **Deployer:** the staffing agency. **Vendor:** same as provider; the agency must not rebrand or substantially modify it, or it becomes the provider (Art 25). | **Vendor (provider):** risk management, data governance and bias testing, technical documentation, logging capability, instructions for use, human-oversight design, accuracy/robustness, QMS, conformity assessment, CE marking, EU database registration. **Agency (deployer, Art 26):** use per instructions; trained, competent overseers; relevant input data; monitor and report serious incidents; keep logs ≥ 6 months; tell applicants a high-risk AI system is used (Art 26(11)); explanation on request (Art 86). Parallel: GDPR DPIA and Art 22 (the human review has to be real), German equal-treatment law (AGG), works-council co-determination on selection guidelines. Timing: Annex III duties apply from 2 Dec 2027 after the Digital Omnibus (Reg. (EU) 2026/1744), but build them into the procurement contract now. | **Approve with controls** |
| **C** - Dutch parcel depot, carton demand forecast | **Minimal risk** | Internal optimisation on aggregate, non-personal data. Not a listed use, no interaction with the public, and a planner decides. It may not even meet the Art 3(1) definition of an "AI system" if it's a plain statistical forecast. | **Trigger:** weekly planning run. **Behaviour:** forecasting model on carton-size shipment totals, lead times, promotions and holidays proposes reorder quantities. **Human point:** inventory planner checks capacity, edits and submits. **Output:** purchase proposal in the existing procurement system. **Logging:** keep forecast vs. actual for accuracy tracking (good practice, not legally required). | **Provider and deployer:** the depot (builds and uses it in-house). **Vendor:** none. | No specific AI Act obligations beyond Art 4 AI literacy. Parallel: confirm the feed really has no shipment-level or customer data (otherwise GDPR applies); don't market the tool externally with accuracy claims you can't back up. | **Approve** |
| **D** - Italian contact centre, agent voice emotion flags | **Prohibited** | Art 5(1)(f) bans AI that infers the **emotions** (irritated, anxious) of people **at work** from **biometric data**, and pitch and cadence are voice biometrics. The only exceptions are medical or safety reasons, which the brief rules out. The supervisor reviewing each flag doesn't cure it: the inference itself is the banned practice. In force since 2 Feb 2025; fines up to €35m or 7% of turnover. | **As proposed:** shift-end batch, acoustic emotion model, named per-employee flags on a dashboard. **Redesign:** the call transcript plus objective call metadata (duration outliers, holds, transfers, repeat contacts, refunds/escalations, script and compliance checks) flags **calls** for coaching review. No emotion inference, no voice biometrics. Agents can self-nominate calls; the dashboard shows team-level trends by default. | **Provider:** the vendor. **Deployer:** the contact centre (and, as the employer, the party liable for using a prohibited practice). | As proposed: stop the pilot and don't deploy. Redesign: if call flags feed **individual performance evaluation**, the system becomes high-risk under Annex III §4(b), so it needs Art 26 deployer duties, worker information before use (Art 26(7)), and logs. Parallel: GDPR DPIA; Italian Workers' Statute Art 4 (remote monitoring of workers needs a union agreement or labour-inspectorate authorisation). | **Deny and redesign** |

## Assumptions I would confirm with each client

- **A:** Does the widget ever handle ticket or refund requests in practice? Is the LLM API's EU data residency acceptable?
- **B:** Is the tool used only for applicants, or also to allocate shifts to existing temp workers? (Both are Annex III §4, but the second also triggers Art 26(7) worker information.)
- **C:** Is the depot building the model itself, or buying a SaaS planner?
- **D:** Is the vendor's "non-emotion" mode (transcript-only) available, or is acoustic analysis built in?
