# EU AI Act Approval Pack — Consulting Response

> For partner review: the intended AI Act category for each case is intentionally omitted. Please classify each case independently based on the facts.

## Case 1

A telecommunications operator wants to reduce bad debt and the cost of servicing customers it considers repeatedly unprofitable. The proposed system would combine payment history, complaint frequency, contract cancellations, device-financing history, call-centre interactions and publicly available online behaviour into a continuously updated customer profile. That profile would influence which customers receive retention offers, flexible payment options, device financing and priority access to customer support. Front-line staff would see the recommendation and could technically override it, although the business expects the score to be followed in most cases. The company argues that the model would simply allow it to focus commercial resources on customers who are more likely to maintain a valuable relationship.

## Case 2

A fast-growing hospitality group receives several thousand applications each month for hotel, restaurant and headquarters roles. It wants to introduce a screening tool that analyses CVs, application-form responses, structured skills assessments and vacancy requirements, then produces a shortlist and a suitability score for each role. The system would also identify which experience, qualifications and career histories tend to correlate with successful hires in similar positions. Recruiters would conduct final interviews, but applications below a configurable threshold would normally not progress to human review. HR staff could reopen an excluded application, although this would not be part of the standard workflow. The company expects the tool to cut initial screening time while making selection more consistent across locations.

## Case 3

A satellite-internet provider already uses an AI voice agent as the first layer of its customer-service operation and is considering expanding the share of contacts it handles. When a customer calls, the system interprets the request and uses basic account information, current service status, recent network incidents and available support history to determine the next step. It can answer routine questions about installation, service availability or basic troubleshooting, or prepare a short summary before routing the customer to the relevant service team. Billing disputes, cancellations, complaints and requests involving changes to commercial terms are transferred to a human employee. Customers can request a human agent at any point, and service staff can correct the AI-generated summary or routing decision.

## Case 4

A large online marketplace wants to make product reviews easier to scan when an item has accumulated hundreds or thousands of comments. The proposed system would analyse the text of published customer reviews and generate a short summary of recurring themes, for example whether buyers repeatedly mention durability, sizing, ease of installation or battery life. The summary would appear next to the existing review distribution and would link users back to the original reviews rather than replacing them. It would not alter product rankings, determine seller eligibility, score individual reviewers or decide whether a review is published. The merchandising team would monitor summary quality and investigate products where generated summaries appear misleading or unstable.
