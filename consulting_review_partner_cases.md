# Consulting review – partner's four cases (Phase 2)

> Reviewer: Murat Çömez · Partner / client: Max · Date: 18 Nov 2026
> Input: partner's unlabelled briefs ([`partner_client_briefs.pdf`](partner_client_briefs.pdf)). Filled in **before** seeing the partner's answer key.

| Case | Likely category | Why this is my first-pass call | Proposed AI architecture | Provider / deployer / vendor | Required obligations or controls | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1 – Smartwatch health data for insurers** | **High-risk – Annex III 5(c)** (borderline Art. 5(1)(c) social scoring) | AI used for risk assessment and pricing of natural persons for **life and health insurance** is explicitly listed in Annex III 5(c). Lifestyle data from a consumer device used to set premiums is close to social scoring (data from an unrelated context leading to unfavourable treatment). | Trigger: insurer requests a score for an applicant who opted in → model computes a health risk score from wearable data → **underwriter reviews** score, drivers and data quality before any premium or eligibility decision → decision letter with explanation → logs of inputs, score, reviewer, override; consent record | **Provider:** smartwatch manufacturer (builds the scoring system and sells it to insurers). **Deployers:** insurers. **Vendor:** cloud/ML platform, possibly a US analytics provider | Provider: Art. 9–15, QMS, conformity assessment, CE marking, EU database registration. Deployer: Art. 26 oversight and logs, **FRIA (Art. 27)**, Art. 86 explanation. GDPR Art. 9(2)(a) explicit consent, Art. 22, DPIA. ePrivacy/TDDDG §25, Data Act. | **Approve with controls**, limited to an opt-in channel; no data sale without explicit, separate consent |
| **2 – Supermarket emotion monitoring** | **Prohibited – Art. 5(1)(f)** | It infers employees' emotions (happiness, frustration, stress) from facial expressions at the **workplace**, not for medical or safety reasons. Prohibited since 2 Feb 2025. | As proposed: cameras → facial emotion model → individual score → manager rankings. **Redesign:** anonymous store-level service metrics (queue time, transaction time), voluntary pulse surveys, aggregated, no biometrics | **Provider:** camera/AI vendor. **Deployer:** supermarket chain | None can make it lawful. Redesign: no biometric inference; if individual performance is evaluated → Annex III 4(b) high-risk; GDPR Art. 9; BetrVG §87(1) Nr. 6 works council | **Deny and redesign** |
| **3 – Database duplicate cleaning** | **Minimal risk** | Data quality tool: no Art. 5 practice, no Annex III area, no interaction with the public, no synthetic content. | Trigger: new record or batch scan → ML similarity matching → high-confidence matches auto-merged **with reversible merge and audit log**; uncertain cases to a data steward → merged record → merge log (before/after, confidence, who) | **Provider:** tool vendor (or the company if built in-house). **Deployer:** the company | No AI Act obligations except Art. 4 AI literacy. GDPR: accuracy (Art. 5(1)(d)), integrity, rectification and erasure workflows, logging; wrong merges of people's records can mix personal data | **Approve** (flag GDPR) |
| **4 – AI furniture marketing** | **Limited risk – Art. 50** | Generative AI creates images and promotional text. Art. 50(2): provider marks output as AI-generated. Art. 50(4): realistic images of products in rooms that could look authentic → deployer discloses. Not Annex III. | Trigger: marketing uploads product photo → GenAI places it in a styled room and drafts copy → **marketer reviews** product accuracy and claims → publish with "AI-generated/enhanced image" label → asset log (prompt, model, approver) | **Provider:** GenAI tool vendor. **Deployer:** furniture retailer | Art. 50(2) marking (vendor), Art. 50(4) disclosure (retailer), Art. 4 literacy. Consumer law (UCPD): the product must look as it really is (colour, size, material). Copyright of training/reference images. | **Approve with controls** |
