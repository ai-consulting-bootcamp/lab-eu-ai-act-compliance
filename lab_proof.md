## Workflow
1. Write 4 hidden briefs (prohibited, high-risk, limited risk, minimal risk).
2. Swap briefs with my partner.
3. Classify each case with the decision rules.
4. Write the approval pack.
5. Hold the client discussion and compare against the answer key.

## One run (input → evidence → output)
- **Input:** partner's Case 2. *"We hire around 400 people a year … an AI tool that reads the CVs … gives every applicant a score. Recruiters would only open the top 20; everyone else gets an automatic 'thank you, but no' email … from a US vendor."*
- **Evidence:**
  - Annex III 4(a) covers filtering applications and evaluating candidates.
  - Auto-rejection without review conflicts with Art. 14 human oversight and GDPR Art. 22.
  - A US vendor means a provider outside the EU, which needs an authorised representative (Art. 22).
  - The Annex III date is 2 Dec 2027, under the Digital Omnibus (Reg. (EU) 2026/1744).
- **Final output:** high-risk → **Approve with controls**. Conditions:
  - no rejection without a recruiter's action
  - no "top performer" training data
  - vendor conformity evidence
  - a works council agreement

## Failure / limitation
- **My own reading error:** I first missed that the Case 2 brief named a US vendor. It's in the last sentence, and it changes the role map (an EU authorised representative is needed). Lesson: read the whole brief for role facts, not just the use case.
- **Limitation of the exercise:** my partner presented the briefs in category order (1 = prohibited … 4 = minimal), so position alone hinted at the tier. The 4/4 match overstates how hard the classification was. My own briefs were shuffled to avoid this.
