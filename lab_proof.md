## 1. Workflow (what I did)
1. Wrote four scenarios, one per outcome (prohibited, high-risk, transparency, minimal), with a private answer key.
2. Removed labels **and shuffled the order** (Client A–D) so the case position does not reveal the tier.
3. Swapped briefs with my partner; reviewed their four cases with the checklist in `consulting_review_partner_cases.md` (Art. 5 → Annex III → Art. 50 → minimal → roles).
4. Wrote the approval pack with a decision per case using the required decision rules.
5. Live review: partner revealed their key, we compared, I recorded changes in the closing note.

## 2. One run – input
Partner's brief "Employee Performance & Satisfaction Monitoring in Supermarkets": AI cameras at checkouts analyse employees' facial expressions and give each one a happiness / frustration / stress score, used for evaluations, rankings and shift assignments.

## 3. Evidence it produced
First-pass call: **Prohibited, Art. 5(1)(f)**: emotion recognition from biometric (facial) data in the workplace, with no medical or safety purpose. Provider: camera/AI vendor; deployer: supermarket. No control can make it lawful.

## 4. Final output
**Deny and redesign:** measure the service, not the person (store-level queue and transaction metrics, anonymous pulse surveys, no biometrics). See Case 2 in [`approval_pack_partner_cases.md`](approval_pack_partner_cases.md).

## 6. Failure or limitation I hit
**Role allocation was harder than classification.** The tiers followed quickly from the facts. Deciding who is provider and who is deployer was less obvious, especially for the smartwatch case: the manufacturer builds the scoring system and sells it to insurers, so it is the provider, and the insurers are deployers with their own FRIA duty. A short brief does not say who develops, who rebrands and who operates the system.
