### Lab | EU AI Act - Use Case Classifier

| Case | Client brief for partner | Intended category in your private answer key | Why you chose it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Store planning pilot | Northline Markets is testing a planning service for its 42 supermarkets after several stores over-ordered fresh food last summer. The service combines three years of sales, supplier lead times, weather, promotions and local-event calendars to suggest orders by store and product. It does not use customer profiles or employee records, and its output goes to buyers rather than directly to shoppers. Store managers can change a recommendation before an order is placed, with the override recorded for the next model update. The CFO wants to know whether the use of AI itself means the pilot must be paused. | - | - |
| Wellbeing subscription journey | BrightPath runs a paid wellbeing app for adults across the EU. After a user has made several mood entries, recorded a few voice notes or connected sleep data, the app estimates when a guided programme might feel timely. The product team has found that sign-ups rise after repeated poor-sleep nights or entries describing that the user is struggling, so the system now selects the prompt’s timing and wording. A one-tap trial becomes an annual subscription unless the user cancels; the same price is shown to everyone. The client says the prompt is labelled |- |-|
| Customer support refresh | SkyWays is revising its online support journey because customers often open several cases about baggage, schedule changes and refunds. A vendor-provided assistant will answer questions in natural language, read the customer’s booking details, and hand complicated refund requests to the existing human team; it cannot approve or deny a refund. The marketing team also plans to use the same vendor’s video generator for short social-media clips showing composite travellers at destinations, with a human editor checking the final claims. The airline wants to know what customers should be told at the point of interaction and what labels, if any, belong on the clips. | - | - |
| Hiring operations workflow | Cedar & Finch is expanding its retail workforce and wants one workflow for seasonal hiring and internal progression. A vendor tool reads application forms, CVs, availability and structured work-sample results, then orders candidates into a recruiter queue; recruiters usually interview only the top fifth but may override the order with a reason. The same platform compares performance-review and training records to suggest employees for promotion, while the final decision remains with a store or regional manager. Cedar & Finch sets the criteria and supplies the data, but the vendor hosts and updates the model, and the HR director assumes the contract makes the vendor responsible for compliance. | - | - |
