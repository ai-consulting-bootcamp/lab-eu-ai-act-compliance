## Consulting response

Share the four briefs below without the private answer key above.

### Client brief 1

- A regional public-service platform is trying to reduce queues and give caseworkers one view of demand for permits, housing support, and recreation bookings.
- The product team proposes a resident profile score based on late utility payments, benefit-claim history, missed appointments, complaint records, and publicly visible social-media activity.
- The score would rank residents from reliable to difficult and carry across those otherwise unrelated services.
- Higher-scoring residents could receive faster appointments and fewer document checks, while lower-scoring residents could be routed to extra verification or lower priority.
- Caseworkers would see the score and normally follow it, with an appeal form and a supervisor able to override it after review.
- Residents would be affected when requesting public services, even though some of the data was originally collected for different purposes.

### Client brief 2

- A nationwide staffing agency fills short-notice warehouse and care-support shifts and needs to reduce its recruiter backlog.
- A vendor proposes an AI service that parses applications, ranks candidates against each vacancy, and recommends who should receive an interview.
- It would use CVs, work history, certificates, availability, language information, and transcripts from recorded screening calls.
- Recruiters could adjust the scoring weights and reject the ranking, but the shortlist would normally be sent to hiring managers automatically.
- Applicants would be affected through access to interviews and paid work, and the agency wants an audit trail of rankings and overrides.
- An external HR-technology vendor would host the model and provide the agency with a dashboard and API.

### Client brief 3

- A mid-sized online home-goods retailer wants to reduce pressure on its customer-support team during seasonal order peaks.
- It plans to add a conversational assistant to the website and mobile app to answer product questions, check order status, and draft return instructions.
- The assistant would retrieve information from the product catalogue, shipping FAQs, order records, and the customer's messages before generating a reply.
- Customers would receive the responses in a natural conversational style and could use the assistant outside normal support hours.
- Human support agents would handle complaints, refunds, and unusual cases, with the assistant able to transfer a conversation to an agent.
- A customer-service platform vendor would host the model while the retailer supplies the content and connects the order-status system.

### Client brief 4

- A 70-person architecture firm is spending too much staff time sorting newsletters, bulk mail, and suspected phishing messages.
- It wants an AI mail filter that labels incoming messages as important, bulk, or likely phishing and moves low-priority messages into review folders.
- The system would use message text, sender and header information, past employee corrections, and the firm's existing allow/block lists.
- Employees could restore a message or change its label, and the IT team would monitor false positives and update the rules.
- No hiring, performance, pay, or client-access decision would be based on the label; the output would only change inbox placement.
- A hosted email-security vendor would run the classifier, while the firm's IT administrator would configure it and review aggregate results.
