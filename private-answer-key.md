# Private answer key (author: Ben Mayer)

Hidden from the partner until the client discussion. The briefs themselves are in [`client-briefs-for-partner.md`](./client-briefs-for-partner.md).

| Case | Client brief for partner (short) | Intended category | Why I chose it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 - Prohibited | Fashion chain entrance cameras pick screen campaigns by estimated age, gender, ethnic heritage and religious observance | **Prohibited** - Art 5(1)(g) | Categorising people from their facial biometrics to infer race/ethnic origin or religious beliefs is banned outright; age/gender alone would not be, which is the trap. |
| 2 - High-risk | Business school auto-scores essays and interviews; top and bottom bands get automatic offers / waitlisting | **High-risk** - Annex III §3(a)-(b) | AI that determines admission to education, and evaluates learning outcomes used to steer admission, is a listed high-risk use; the auto-decided bands make human oversight the key weakness. |
| 3 - Limited / transparency | Tourism board campaign with an AI-generated presenter and AI-altered footage of real places | **Limited risk** - Art 50(2) and 50(4) | Synthetic video of a realistic person and real places that could pass as authentic is a deepfake: the provider must mark it machine-readably and the deployer must disclose it as AI-generated. |
| 4 - Minimal | Publisher auto-tags its internal photo archive, archivists approve tags | **Minimal risk** | Internal content tagging with human acceptance, no decisions about people and no listed use; only the Art 4 AI-literacy duty and parallel GDPR/contract points apply. |

## Deliberate "hints" and traps

- Case 1: no identification and no stored faces. This is meant to tempt a reviewer into "anonymous, so fine". Art 5(1)(g) still applies, because inferring sensitive traits is what's banned, not identifying people.
- Case 2: humans can override, but in practice only the middle band gets read. Tests whether the reviewer spots that oversight is nominal.
- Case 3: the marketing team approves every clip. Human review does **not** remove the deepfake disclosure duty. The "sunnier, emptier beaches" detail raises a parallel consumer-protection (misleading advertising) issue.
- Case 4: photos contain people. The reviewer should flag GDPR for identifiable persons without escalating the AI Act tier.
