# Simulated client discussion

This is a scripted learning exercise generated with AI assistance. The questions, clarifications, and client responses below are fictional. No colleague supplied them, no live discussion took place, and no actual sign-off is recorded. The student reports having informed the instructor about using synthetic material.

| Case | Intended category | Simulated consultant classification | Scripted client response | Consequence for the recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | Limited risk / transparency | Limited risk / transparency | Requests approval without slowing replies through manual review. Confirms staff can take over uncertain enquiries. | Approve with controls. Add an accessible AI disclosure at first interaction, source-grounded answers, escalation, and provider assessment of machine-readable output marking. |
| B | High-risk | High-risk | Challenges the classification because a credit officer signs every decision. Accepts that officers routinely rely on the score. | Approve with controls, conditional on evidence and applicable deadlines. Keep the classification and specify meaningful oversight, reasons and appeals, a FRIA, and data protection assessment. |
| C | Prohibited | Prohibited | Asks whether student consent and teacher override would permit the proposal. Accepts removing emotion inference. | Deny and redesign. Replace the proposal with voluntary self-reported lesson feedback and teacher-led follow-up. Assess the replacement separately before launch. |
| D | Minimal risk | Minimal risk | Accepts the proposal but asks whether raw customer order histories could be added later. Agrees to retain aggregate inputs for this scope. | Approve the stated scope. Add a change review before introducing personal data or person-level decisions. |

## What changed after the client discussion?

In this simulation, the classifications remained stable while the operating requirements became more precise. Case A gained an explicit escalation route. Case B gained a documented human-review process and evidence gates. Case C changed to self-reported feedback without biometric emotion inference. Case D gained a review trigger for changes to the input data.

The four matching classifications are an internal consistency check of constructed examples. They provide no measurement of independent reviewer accuracy. The scripted acceptance of recommendations is part of the exercise.
