# Simulated partner briefs

These are fictional stand-ins for incoming partner work. No real exchange or independent partner assessment took place.

## A · ElbeStay

A hotel group has frequent front-desk turnover and wants managers to use one coaching rubric. A vendor proposes using facial expressions and vocal tones from recorded staff role-plays to flag individual employees as anxious, irritated or comfortable. Managers can override each flag before assigning coaching. The purpose is service quality, not medical care or safety.

## B · Dockline Staffing

A private staffing agency has a large pool of applicants for industrial maintenance roles. A vendor’s system uses CVs, certifications and a job-specific skills matrix to score applicants. The dashboard sorts candidates and shows the strongest 20 first. A recruiter can inspect everyone and makes the final interview decision. In practice, staff start their review from the displayed shortlist.

## C · MuseumPass

A museum pass operator wants to reduce questions about opening hours and ticket rules. Its own site will offer a conversational assistant based on an external language model and approved FAQ pages. Visitors type free-form questions and receive generated answers. The assistant cannot access accounts or issue refunds. Staff publish the FAQ and handle escalations without pre-reading every answer.

## D · RegisterMatch

A legal research operations team wants to reduce duplicates in an internal register of incorporated companies. A nightly AI batch compares company names, register courts and registration numbers. It returns IDs and match scores to an internal queue. An analyst checks source records before a merge. The system excludes sole traders, officer names and other personal data. The output is not public and is not used to rate companies or people.
