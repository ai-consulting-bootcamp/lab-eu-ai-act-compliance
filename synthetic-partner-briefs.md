# Synthetic partner briefs

These fictional briefs replace unavailable partner input. The student reports having informed the instructor. All scenarios were generated with AI assistance. The same assistant generated the supporting key and debrief, so this exercise cannot demonstrate independent blind classification.

## Case A

- A Spanish furniture retailer loses customers who cannot find delivery and assembly information outside office hours.
- It wants a conversational assistant on its website to answer questions about products and published delivery terms.
- The assistant generates responses using the retailer's approved catalogue and delivery policy, plus the visitor's typed question.
- Prospective customers use the answers to plan purchases. The assistant cannot approve credit, change prices, or complete purchases.
- Staff maintain the source documents and handle escalations, but do not approve each response before display.
- The retailer commissions the application under its own name and uses a third-party language-model API.

## Case B

- A French consumer lender wants to shorten the review time for personal loan applications.
- It plans to license a vendor-branded system that predicts repayment likelihood and recommends a lending limit.
- Inputs include verified income, existing debts, repayment history, and the applicant's requested amount.
- Individual applicants may receive a lower limit or be declined after an officer reviews the recommendation.
- A credit officer can override the score and must record the reasons, although the score remains central to the assessment.
- The lender wants to assess affordability and creditworthiness. It already runs a separate transaction-fraud service.

## Case C

- A German language school wants teachers to identify adult students who seem disengaged during online classes.
- It plans to license software that analyses webcam facial movements and estimates boredom, frustration, and enthusiasm.
- Each student's estimated emotional state appears beside their name in the teacher dashboard.
- Teachers use the dashboard to decide whom to question and whom to refer for a participation discussion.
- A teacher can dismiss a flag and the software does not assign grades.
- The school describes the purpose as teaching engagement and classroom management, with no medical or safety function.

## Case D

- A Portuguese wholesaler has uneven stocks of cleaning supplies across its distribution centres.
- It wants its internal analytics team to train a model that predicts weekly demand for each product.
- Inputs are weekly product-level sales totals, stock levels, supplier lead times, and the promotion calendar.
- The output recommends replenishment quantities to inventory planners and affects the availability of goods.
- Planners review capacity and budget, change quantities when necessary, and approve purchase orders themselves.
- The system uses aggregate product data, produces numeric forecasts, and has no customer or employee scoring function.
